Track 3 · AI Filmmaking Workflow

The tools, and where to each one

Five modules to move from theory to running the workshop: the pipeline map, followed by one tool per lesson — Freepik to build the scene, Seedance for movement, Kling for the camera, Luma and Runway for finishing. Each lesson comes with prompts ready to copy.

0 of 24 sections read in this track

The first two courses established the mindset: why a scene makes an impact, how space guides the eye, how light and depth build meaning. This track takes that foundation and puts it to work in the machines. Here you build the workflow from start to finish—from the still image to the finished clip—and learn to choose the right tool for each stage, because none of them does everything well. Module 3.1 is the map: image → motion → refinement → editing, and when to call on each one. Module 3.2 builds the scene in nodes in Freepik. Section 3.3 brings the frame to life with the physics of Seedance. Section 3.4 directs the camera in Kling, with twenty-five cataloged movements. And 3.5 closes with Luma and Runway, where you direct by talking instead of writing commands. The rule that runs through everything remains: the tool executes your direction—it doesn’t invent it.

3.3Motion Generation in Seedance

The frame comes alive — with physics, not adjectives. The Seedance understands force, timing, and weight, and the lesson teaches you to think like a director: define the force that initiates the movement, the sequence of events (large → small → particles), the camera e a human reaction — always showing the emotion through the body, never naming it. With ready-to-use motion prompts, measured in seconds and layers.

The camera as an instrument of emotion. In Kling, you learn the technical—and, more importantly, narrative—difference between dolly, zoom e dolly zoom, and a catalog of more than twenty-five movements, each with its own intention: moving closer for intimacy, pulling back for isolation, a Dutch angle for tension, a crane shot for scale. Module 3.5·5 sections · ~18 min

3.5Luma and Runway integration

The finishing touch, directed through conversation. A Luma (with Uni-1) is a filmmaking environment where you talk as you would with a human collaborator instead of writing commands: the system remembers the project context, so character, light, and tone stay consistent across shots. The three-step workflow — create the character, direct the shot, animate — and the ability to compare outputs (Kling, Ray) and choose the one that serves your vision. This wraps up the track's workshop.

▸ Going deeper: why one tool per lesson optional

The happy path is linear: 3.1, 3.2, 3.3, 3.4, 3.5. This layer explains the learning path’s design—and can be skipped.

The course is organized by pipeline phase, not by brand. Module 3.1 gives you the map; each following lesson explores one phase in depth using the tool that best represents it. That doesn’t mean you need them all: the logic you learn in each one—building the scene in nodes, directing movement through physics, choosing camera movement based on intent, refining through conversation—applies to any equivalent generator. Tools change names every few months; the judgment of direction, not.

That’s why Track 3 is the course’s turning point: it’s where the mindset from Tracks 1 and 2 becomes concrete practice, with prompts you can copy, adapt, and iterate on. The following tracks — camera language, VFX, performance, narrative structure — assume you already know how to work in the workshop.

Physics, not adjectives. Seedance responds to force, timing, and weight — not words like “epic.” Describe what moves, in what order, and over how much time, and the movement will appear.
Definition
Dolly vs. zoom. A dolly moves the camera through space (creating parallax and depth); a zoom changes the lens (flattening the image). Confusing the two is the most common reason AI video “looks fake.”
Going deeper
The 3.5 shift: you don't write commands, you direct. Speaking in natural language lets AI understand intent, remember context between steps, and keep you in the director’s seat.
