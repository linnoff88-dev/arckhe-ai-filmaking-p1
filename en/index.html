Why this exists·Read before the index

§The “wow” isn’t the explosion — it’s the control of perception

Most of what’s taught about AI video is a race to create effects: explosions, giant waves, destruction.1 But a scene's impact doesn't come from the effect — it comes from control what the viewer sees, when they see it, from what distance, and at what pace. AI amplifies what’s already there: if there’s structure, it amplifies the structure; if there’s chaos, it amplifies the chaos.

That’s why the course starts with the perceptual foundation — the three levels of impact and the algorithm every strong scene follows — and only then moves on to the tools. They’re seven tracks: from the foundations of visual language to the real production pipeline with Seedance, Kling, Runway, Luma, and Freepik. No promises of shortcuts; just a clearer way to think about moving images.

▸ Going deeper: why start with theory optional

Optional layer—the index below is enough to get started. The technology has removed the budget barrier: what once required a crew, studio, and VFX now fits on a laptop. But this shifts the competition to visual language — when everyone has the generator, the winner is whoever understands composition, light, scale, rhythm, and movement. Learning the tool without the foundation produces pretty, empty images; learning the foundation turns the generator into a system.

Where this material comes from

This is a study notebook: an editorial adaptation of the course AI Filmmaking — Part 1, reorganized into INEMA.CLUB’s reading format—rewritten in clear Portuguese, with illustrations and ready-to-use prompts. The goal is to study and practice cinematic language applied to video generators.

The content prioritizes the because of each visual decision; the tools mentioned (Seedance, Kling, Runway, Luma, Freepik, Midjourney) evolve quickly, so treat prompts as starting points for iteration, not as fixed recipes.

The most honest approach is simply to start and see if the tone works for you. Module 0 opens on a reading page, with no sign-up—and you can stop after the first section if it’s not for you.

Definition
Perception. The “wow” happens when the brain briefly loses its ability to predict what comes next — triggered by scale, acceleration, contrast in lighting, depth, and danger. Cinema is about controlling that.
Going deeper
Expensive scene vs. cheap scene: the expensive one has clear spatial geography, readable composition, depth, controlled lighting, and rhythm. Scale without clarity is noise; clarity with scale is epic.
